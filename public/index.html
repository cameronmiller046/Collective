        Rooted & Crowned Collective was not created from theory alone. It was created from a life that required me to learn how to begin again—more than once.

        My journey has moved through love and loss, marriage and divorce, motherhood and advocacy, raising children with unique needs, rebuilding relationships, rediscovering myself, and learning that sometimes the life we planned and the life we are given are not the same.

        For years, I knew how to survive. But eventually, survival was no longer enough. I wanted to understand the patterns beneath my experiences, reconnect with the parts of myself I had lost along the way, and become intentional about the woman I was becoming.

        That journey became the foundation of Rooted & Crowned.

        I began looking at growth from the Root up—our foundation, creativity, personal power, relationships, voice, vision, and purpose. But I also realized that awareness could not be the destination. What we discover about ourselves must eventually become part of how we live.

        That became the heart of my philosophy:

        “Grounded Roots. Crown Driven.” Heal from the Root Up. Live from the Crown Down.

        Rooted & Crowned Collective grew from that understanding—a space where we can acknowledge where we’ve been without allowing it to become the conclusion of our story. A place where reflection becomes action, our voices matter, and what once challenged us can become wisdom for what we build next.

        I don’t stand here as someone who has lived a perfect life. I stand here as a woman who has lived, learned, rebuilt, created, and continued becoming.

        And now I create spaces for other women to do the same.

            For Beginners

            What is the Root Chakra, actually?

            "Chakra" is a Sanskrit word that roughly means "wheel" or "disc." In yogic and spiritual traditions, the body is described as having seven major energy centers running from the base of the spine to the crown of the head — each one associated with a different theme of human experience. You don't need to believe in energy centers for this framework to be useful; think of it as a map for self-reflection, the same way a personality framework or a set of life-stage theories gives you language for something you already sense but haven't named.

            The Root Chakra sits at the very base of that map — literally the base of the spine — and it's associated with the most foundational human questions: Am I safe? Do I belong? Can I trust? Am I supported? Every other chakra in the system (creativity, confidence, love, voice, intuition, purpose) is described as resting on top of this one. If the foundation is shaky, everything built on it tends to wobble too — which is why this is where the whole Root-to-Crown journey begins.

            In plain terms: this course is about the beliefs you formed — often before you were old enough to question them — about whether the world is safe, whether you're allowed to trust people, and whether there will be enough. Those beliefs are still quietly running in the background of your adult decisions, and this course helps you find them and, where they're no longer true, replace them.

            5 & 6. The Biology of the Root Experience

            What the body and brain are doing.

            There is no scientifically established "Root Chakra center" in the brain. Chakra traditions belong to spiritual, yogic and subtle-energy systems, not to established neuroscience — but several biological systems are highly relevant when we talk about safety, threat, memory and regulation. You don't need a science background for this section; here's each one in plain terms, with an everyday analogy:

            • Amygdala — helps detect emotionally significant cues and participates in threat learning. Think of it like a smoke alarm: it's fast and reactive, but it can't tell burnt toast from a house fire — it just knows to sound the alarm.
            • Hippocampus — supports memory and context, helping the brain tell "this happened then" from "this is happening now." It's like the timestamp on a photo — without it, an old memory can feel like it's happening in the present.
            • Medial/prefrontal cortex — supports regulation, planning, and flexible decision-making. This is the "reasonable adult in the room" part of the brain, which can talk the smoke alarm down once it has a moment to catch up.
            • Hypothalamus and HPA axis — coordinate hormonal stress responses, including cortisol. Think of it as the body's internal alarm system that decides how loud and how long to sound the alert.
            • Autonomic nervous system — sympathetic activation supports mobilization (gas pedal); parasympathetic processes support rest and recovery (brake pedal). Most of us are more practiced at the gas pedal than the brake.
            • Interoception/insula — helps represent internal bodily states like breath, tension and heartbeat. This is the sense that lets you notice you're clenching your jaw before you consciously decide to relax it.

            Research on childhood adversity reports associations between threat exposure and differences in these systems — but these are population-level associations, not destiny for any one individual.

            A Guided Practice

            Grounding: bringing your body into the present moment.

            This is a short, body-based practice you can return to any time — in this course, before a hard conversation, or in the middle of an ordinary day. It works by giving your nervous system clear, present-tense evidence that right now, in this moment, you are safe.

            1. Find a seat, or stand with both feet flat on the floor.
            2. Take a slow breath in for a count of four, and let it out for a count of six — the longer exhale is what tells your body it can stand down.
            3. Press your feet into the floor and actually feel the contact — the texture, the temperature, the solidness underneath you.
            4. Name, out loud or silently, five things you can see around you right now.
            5. Repeat the breath one more time: in for four, out for six.
            6. Say to yourself: "Right now, in this moment, I am safe."

            This practice doesn't erase what made you feel unsafe in the past, and it isn't a substitute for professional support if you're in crisis. It's simply a tool for coming back to the present when your body is reacting to an old danger that isn't happening right now.

            12. Root-Up and Crown-Down Healing

            Elevation doesn't mean escaping the Root.

            Root-up healing asks: what foundation must be stabilized so I can safely grow? Crown-down healing asks: what higher truth, spiritual value, purpose or vision should now guide the way I rebuild that foundation?

            Root Up: Safety → emotion/creation → power → love → voice → vision → spiritual connection.

            Crown Down: Meaning/faith → vision → truthful expression → compassionate connection → empowered action → creative life → grounded daily practice.

            Elevation is not escaping the Root. Elevation is bringing higher awareness back down into the way I live, choose, spend, love, speak, rest and protect my peace.

            Rooted Together

            Rooted Together. Crowning Generations.

            Because when we strengthen one person, we strengthen a family. When we strengthen families, we strengthen communities. And when communities rise, generations change.

            At Rooted & Crowned Collective, we believe every person deserves the opportunity to feel seen, supported, empowered, and capable of discovering what is possible for their life.

            Rooted Together is the community-impact arm of that vision — a place where compassion becomes action. Our purpose extends beyond individual transformation. We want to help create stronger foundations for children, families, caregivers, neurodivergent individuals, and communities that may need additional resources, advocacy, encouragement, education, or simply someone willing to stand beside them.

            This isn’t only about giving. It’s about belonging.

            It’s about recognizing that none of us grows alone — and that sometimes one person choosing to care can become the beginning of an entirely different future.

            Why we are Rooted Together

            A tree grows strong from what happens underground.

            Roots intertwine. They share space. They endure storms. They create the foundation that allows something greater to rise. We believe communities work the same way: when people have access to support, knowledge, advocacy, mentorship, wellness resources, creative expression, and meaningful community, possibilities begin to expand. Through Rooted Together, our goal is to help cultivate those possibilities. Our impact will center around:

            Neurodivergent Children & Families

            Supporting initiatives that promote inclusion, understanding, advocacy, family resources, educational opportunities, and environments where neurodivergent children can be recognized for their strengths — not defined solely by their challenges.

            Parents & Caregivers

            Creating spaces where parents and caregivers can find education, encouragement, resources, community, and support while navigating the responsibilities of caring and advocating for those they love.

            Youth Mentorship & Empowerment

            Helping young people develop confidence, identity, creativity, emotional awareness, communication skills, and the courage to imagine possibilities beyond their current circumstances.

            Healing & Wellness Access

            Expanding opportunities for communities to experience wellness education and supportive practices that encourage reflection, emotional awareness, self-expression, and personal development.

            Families & Communities

            Supporting community-centered initiatives designed to strengthen connections across generations and create environments where people can learn, grow, heal, and thrive together.

            More than a cause

            A generational commitment.

            We don’t want impact to end with the person standing in front of us. We want it to travel.

            From a child who finally feels understood…
            to the parent who learns how to advocate for them…
            to the sibling who develops greater compassion…
            to the educator who begins seeing ability differently…
            to the community that becomes more inclusive because someone was willing to start the conversation.

            That is how change becomes generational. We strengthen the root so the next generation has somewhere stronger to stand. And then we remind them that they were always worthy of the crown.

            A personal note from Coach Kia

            Founder, Rooted & Crowned Collective

            There Are Some Missions You Choose. And Others Life Places Directly in Your Hands.

            For me, advocacy became personal long before it became part of Rooted & Crowned Collective.

            As a parent raising and advocating for neurodivergent children, I have experienced what it means to fight to make sure your children are seen, heard, understood, educated, included, supported, and given meaningful opportunities to reach their potential.

            I understand the questions. I understand the paperwork. I understand the meetings, evaluations, systems, and decisions. I understand what it feels like to sit in rooms where decisions are being made about someone you love and know that you must be willing to speak — even when your voice may be the only one speaking.

            But I have also witnessed something extraordinary:

            Possibility.

            I have learned why we cannot measure a person’s future only by what they struggle with today.

            Our children are more than diagnoses, labels, evaluations, behaviors, statistics, or expectations placed upon them. They are:

            ThinkersCreatorsProblem-solversArtistsDreamersInnovators

            And individuals with their own ways of experiencing, understanding, and contributing to this world.

            My experiences as a parent have helped shape something I now carry beyond my own family: a desire to advocate for greater understanding, inclusion, access, support, and opportunity for neurodivergent children and their families in communities everywhere.

            I cannot personally walk every family’s journey. But through Rooted & Crowned Collective and Infinite Possibilities – Rooted Together, we can help create spaces where fewer families feel they have to walk those journeys alone.

            We can create opportunities for families to connect with resources, education, advocacy, supportive communities, and experiences that recognize potential rather than defining people by limitations.

            This is where my story becomes part of a greater responsibility to one another.

            Where personal experience becomes advocacy.
            Where advocacy creates connection.
            Where connection strengthens community.
            Where community creates possibility.
            And where possibility can change generations.

            My hope is not to create a world where every child becomes the same. My hope is to help create a world big enough for every child to become fully themselves.
